Why “doing AI” makes your token costs explode

Every task moves from open-ended exploration to a repeatable process. AI transformation means moving each task off frontier models once it crosses that threshold, so token cost per task falls over time instead of exploding.

Summary

If an organization is just “doing AI,” it throws the best model it can afford at every task and its token costs explode. Every task starts as open-ended exploration and narrows into a predictable, repeatable process. Frontier models are the right tool at the exploration end. Once a task crosses the threshold to scalability, you get better and cheaper results by encoding your organizational context outside the model and running the work on smaller, faster, sometimes deterministic software. AI transformation means tracking where every task sits, so token cost per task goes down over time, not up.

Key points

  • All problem-solving follows the same shape: wide exploration that narrows into a repeatable process.
  • Most teams perform 50 to 100 distinct tasks. Tasks roll up into workflows, workflows into roles, roles into teams.
  • Frontier models are good at exploration, but too expensive and worse at tight, repeatable work.
  • Encode organizational context outside the model, then execute with smaller, faster, more deterministic software.
  • Managing token spend is the business’s job, not IT’s. Done well, cost per task falls over time.

The shape of all problem-solving

If you’re just doing AI rather than doing a proper AI transformation, you’re going to see your token costs explode. Here’s why.

Running an innovation team, I’ve seen firsthand that all problem-solving follows the same basic pattern. You start with a wide range of possibilities, but as you experiment and learn what works, the space gets narrower until something that started as an open-ended exploration becomes a predictable, repeatable process.

Every task has its own triangle

We’ve measured this with dozens of teams, and most have somewhere between 50 and 100 distinct tasks they regularly perform. Each of those tasks has its own little triangle. And because workflows are just groups of tasks, roles are just groups of workflows, and teams are just groups of roles, you can call this triangle the general shape of all knowledge work.

Where frontier models fit, and where they don’t

Frontier models are really good at the wide end. But once a task crosses to the other end, you need it to be tight, repeatable, and predictable. And frontier models aren’t just too expensive for that, they’re bad at it. You get better output by encoding your organizational context outside the model, and using smaller, faster, sometimes even more deterministic software to execute on it.

Why “doing AI” blows the token budget

If your organization is just doing AI, nobody is keeping track of any of this. You’re just taking the same work you do today and throwing the best model you can afford at it. Don’t blame IT for capping your token budget. This is your job to manage, not theirs.

What AI transformation actually means

That’s why AI transformation is so different from doing AI. It means knowing where every one of those triangles is, and noticing when each task crosses the threshold from exploration to scalability. If you’re doing this right, token cost per task goes down over time, not up.
